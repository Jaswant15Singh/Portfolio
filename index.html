About Me

Hello! I’m Brandon San, a proud Cambodian-American passionate about promoting diversity and inclusion in both the medical field and local communities. As a first-generation college student, navigating higher education has been a new experience for both me and my family. I am currently in my third year at the University of Washington, pursuing a degree in Biology with minors in Chemistry, Neuroscience, and Health Studies. On and off campus, I take on active leadership roles, including overseeing a program of 400+ interns at Swedish Medical Center as the Assistant Director of Administration. Additionally, I mentor students through the Filipino Association of Health Careers (FAHC), volunteer as the Lead Inpatient Pharmacy Assistant at EvergreenHealth Medical Center, and serve as the Founding Executive Officer of UW KhSA KAREs (Khmer Student Association Khmer Advocates for Resilience in Education). I am continually seeking professional opportunities that allow me to further develop and apply my leadership skills.

Core Values

Ambition

To me, this means pushing boundaries and challenging the current ideas and perspectives we hold. It’s about setting ambitious goals and striving to surpass them. Throughout my leadership journey, I’ve consistently pushed myself to grow, adapting and rising to new challenges. This mindset has been crucial in opening doors to opportunities and has led me to where I am today.

Resilience

For me, this is about the Cambodian community. It’s about asking, how much do you truly want something, and how far are you willing to go to overcome obstacles? It’s about strengthening a community that was devastated by genocide and empowering each other to rise above adversity. This is deeply important to me because this concept represents both my community and my identity.

Passion

To me, this is what fuels my motivation to get out of bed every morning. Passion isn’t just a single thing; it’s the driving force that gives me endless desire and enthusiasm. This is important because when I’m passionate about something, I’m willing to go to great lengths to achieve my goals.

Unique Leadership Identities

Team Player

A collaborative workplace is a vital part of my job. I believe the team recognizes the value of working together, as having a shared goal gives everyone a meaningful reason to contribute. It’s where diverse ideas come together to solve complex problems. Learning to collaborate effectively is essential, both in academic and professional settings. That’s why encouraging people from diverse backgrounds to enter fields like this fosters greater collaboration and leads to better problem-solving. As an Assistant Director, I’m fortunate to collaborate with various departments within Swedish, including Recruitment and Training. Working closely with other Assistant Directors allows us to leverage each other’s strengths and successfully tackle large projects and tasks as a team.

Mentor

I believe mentorship is crucial to the success of anyone entering the professional field. It provides meaningful professional socialization and personal support that facilitates success in both graduate school and life. Through mentorship, I’ve had the opportunity to improve my communication and soft skills, building valuable relationships as both a mentor and mentee during my undergraduate years. As a mentor, I’ve been able to share my knowledge and experiences while learning from others. As a mentee, I’ve gained invaluable insights academically and professionally, applying them to different aspects of my life.

Innovator

I believe being an innovator is a quality I embody. I have a natural tendency to identify pain points and seek ways to resolve them, always striving for maximum efficiency. In any successful leadership team, having an innovator is essential—they bring fresh ideas that keep operations and processes evolving. Continuous improvement is key to driving progress in a company, and innovation is something I deeply value in the professional field. I’m always looking for ways to streamline workflows, both for myself and my colleagues. Some examples of my contributions include developing trackers, fliers, and processes that enhance our efficiency and allow us to focus on more meaningful work.

“Why” do what I do?





n an ever-evolving world enriched by diversity, embracing and understanding others is essential to acknowledging each individual and their unique journey. In every meeting I attend, I constantly reflect on how to foster inclusion. Whose voices or perspectives are present? And if they aren’t being heard, how can I help change that? My commitment to inclusion and equity fuels my passion for serving my community and amplifying underrepresented voices in the healthcare industry.

Future Goals


As a strong advocate for diversity and inclusion in the medical field, I am passionate about bridging the gap between underserved communities and healthcare services. My aspiration is to become a pharmacist, and through my experiences, I have seen firsthand how members of Southeast Asian communities often delay seeking medical care until illness strikes. This tendency, in my view, stems from a lack of awareness around mental health and preventive care. Since traditional Eastern medicine is often the first line of treatment for these communities, I aim to integrate both Eastern and Western practices to provide more accessible and holistic care, while breaking down cultural and language barriers along the way.
